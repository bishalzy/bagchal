About Baghchal

Quick Definition: What is Baghchal?

Baghchal (Nepali: बाघचाल, meaning "Move of the Tigers") is a traditional, ancient two-player asymmetric strategy board game from Nepal. One player controls 4 predatory Tigers that attempt to capture 5 goats by jumping over them, while the other player commands a defensive herd of 20 Goats working together to trap and immobilize all 4 tigers.

Translating directly to "Move of the Tigers", Baghchal is a celebrated traditional Nepali board game that dates back centuries. Deeply rooted in the Himalayan lifestyle, this asymmetric strategy game pits cunning predators against a resilient herd. In this digital adaptation, players can experience the iconic struggle of four tigers attempting to hunt their prey, while twenty goats work together to gridlock them.

History and Cultural Heritage

This ancient board game from Nepal has been played in the Himalayan regions for hundreds of years. Villagers would draw the 5×5 grid on stone slabs or soil and play in community gathering places called chautaris. Today, Baghchal has gained global recognition, documented in cultural archives such as Wikipedia's Bagh-Chal Entry, and analyzed by game theory researchers worldwide.

Playing strategy games like Baghchal online is great for both kids and adults. It helps practice critical thinking, planning, and patience. Unlike chess, a game of Baghchal typically takes only 15 to 30 minutes, making it a quick and fun way to learn about Nepali culture!
